## English (U.S.)

### Subtitle

Share files with your phone

### Promotional text

Send photos, videos, files and copied text between your Android phone and your Mac — straight over your Wi-Fi, end-to-end encrypted. No cable, no cloud, no account.

### Keywords

phone,file transfer,share,photos,videos,send,receive,wifi,bluetooth,nearby,clipboard,local,sync

### Description

Dewlet connects your Mac and your Android phone. Photos, videos, files and copied text go straight from one to the other over your Wi-Fi — no cable, no cloud, no messages to yourself.

FROM PHONE TO MAC
• Share from any app on the phone and pick your Mac: the files are in your Downloads a moment later.
• Choose what each phone may save without asking — photos and videos, files under 100 MB, or everything.
• A notification tells you when files arrive, with Show in Finder.

FROM MAC TO PHONE
• Start dragging files anywhere and your phone slides in at the edge of the screen: drop them on it.
• Or use Share › Dewlet in Finder, Photos, Safari and other apps.
• The phone wakes up for the files by itself, even when Dewlet isn't open on it.

TEXT AND LINKS
• Send the clipboard to your phone from the menu, or a page from Safari with Share.
• Text from the phone lands on the Mac's clipboard; links open with a click.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
• Files go directly between your devices, end-to-end encrypted, and never through a server.
• No account, no ads, no analytics. Dewlet for Mac collects no data.
• A phone and a Mac pair once with a code shown on both screens; after that only your own devices can connect.

STAYS OUT OF THE WAY
• Lives in the menu bar: a drop that fills up while files come and go.
• Knows when your phone is nearby, and keeps a transfer waiting until it is.
• Opens at login if you want it to.

REQUIREMENTS
• An Android phone (Android 10 or later) with Dewlet for Android from Google Play.
• Bluetooth and Wi-Fi on both devices, on the same network or the phone's hotspot.

## App Review

- Sign-in required: no.
- Notes (while Dewlet for Android is in closed testing):

> Dewlet transfers files between this Mac and the user's own phone. The companion app, Dewlet for Android, is in closed testing on Google Play before its public release, so the attached video shows the whole flow end to end: https://youtu.be/IsIvW3kN3sc
>
> To try it with a phone (Android 10+): join https://groups.google.com/g/dewlet-testers with a Google account, then open the Google Play testing link https://play.google.com/apps/testing/com.zepponapps.localdrop and install Dewlet.
>
> Pair: click the drop in the menu bar and turn on "Visible to new devices"; on the phone tap "Add a Mac" and confirm the same code on both screens. Then share a photo from the phone to the Mac. To send from the Mac, start dragging a file: a small tab appears at the right edge of the screen; drag the file onto it and drop it on the phone that slides out.
>
> No account or sign-in. Launch at login stays off until the user turns it on in the menu or in Settings. The privacy policy is linked in Settings › General › About.
>
> Network server entitlement: the phone connects to the Mac, so Dewlet listens for incoming TCP connections on the local network and advertises that port over Bonjour (_localdrop._tcp, declared in NSBonjourServices). Every file, photo or text the phone sends arrives through this listener; without com.apple.security.network.server the Mac cannot receive anything. A new phone can pair only while the user has turned on "Visible to new devices" and confirmed the same code on both screens; transfers are end-to-end encrypted.

## App Review: 20 October 2026, build 0.1.1 (4), network server entitlement

Automated check: com.apple.security.network.server without matching functionality. The
entitlement stays: the Mac receives through its own TCP listener (BSD sockets in
`TCPListener.swift`, published over Bonjour), which a static scan for NWListener does not see.
Answered in Resolution Center and added to the Notes above:

> Dewlet needs com.apple.security.network.server because the Mac is the receiving side of every
> transfer. The app opens a TCP listening socket on the local network and advertises it over
> Bonjour as _localdrop._tcp (declared in NSBonjourServices). The user's paired phone discovers
> this service and connects to it to send files, photos and copied text to the Mac; without the
> entitlement the Mac cannot accept these incoming connections and receives nothing. A new phone
> can pair only while the user has turned on "Visible to new devices" and confirmed the same code
> on both screens, and transfers are end-to-end encrypted. The listener is implemented with BSD
> sockets and DNS-SD rather than Network.framework, which may be why the automated analysis did
> not detect it. We have added this explanation to the App Review Information notes.
